From form to first shift

Every new driver answers the same first question, whatever their nationality. The answer decides how they are checked. Here is who does each step.

  1. 1Driver

    Opens their onboarding link and says which applies: British citizen, Irish citizen, or another nationality with permission to work.

  2. 2Driver

    British or Irish citizen: picks the original document they’ll bring to the check. The form never asks them for a share code.

  3. 3Driver

    Another nationality: gives their share code and date of birth for the Home Office online check, or taps I can’t get a share code.

  4. 4Driver

    Adds a selfie, NI proof and their licence, and signs a declaration naming your company.

  5. 5Automatic

    The driver shows as Check pending on Right to Work, with their answers attached: the document they’ll bring, or a share code ready to copy.

  6. 6You

    An owner records the matching check: originals in person for British and Irish citizens, the online check for a share code, or the Employer Checking Service.

  7. 7You

    Documents: see the originals with the driver there, and copy them. Online: save the Home Office profile before induction, then match the driver to its photo.

  8. 8Automatic

    Pass locks the record with the checker’s name and the date of the check. Training, promotion and the rota open.

  9. 9You

    Time-limited permission sets a follow-up date. Record the next check before it: from that day only OFF can be set, and planned days clear at 00:05.

Why it meets the new law

From 1 October 2026 the illegal working penalty covers self-employed drivers, at up to £60,000 per driver. It applies to every driver engaged from that date, whatever their nationality. The only defence is a statutory excuse: a Home Office check, done before work starts.

Who needs a check

A new driver, from 1 OctoberYes, every one, British and Irish citizens included. Theirs is a document check, not a share code: an owner sees the original in person.
A driver of another nationalityYes. Their share code and date of birth go to the Home Office online check. Without a share code, the Employer Checking Service.
A driver engaged before 1 OctoberThe new law doesn’t ask for a new check. They keep working on a Legacy record, with their old right to work details carried over.
A driver who left and comes backYes. Coming back is a new engagement, so they need a new check. A Legacy record never covers it.
A driver with time-limited permissionYes, again before their follow-up date. British and Irish citizens never need a follow-up check: their right doesn’t expire.

Connect makes the Home Office check the only way onto the rota. The statutory excuse still rests on your checker genuinely seeing the originals, in person. This describes how Connect works; it isn’t legal advice.

How to use it

Setting it upBefore your company is switched on

  1. You

    Open Admin › Right to work and read the dry run: everyone the nightly check would take off the rota.

  2. You

    Record a check for anyone engaged from 1 October. Set a Block legacy drivers from date only if you want one.

  3. Connect

    We switch your company on with you. The go-live date is stamped, and earlier rota days are never gated.

  4. Automatic

    Drivers engaged before 1 October carry a Legacy record and keep working.

Then, for every new driver:

  1. 1

    Send the driver their onboarding link from Onboarding. British and Irish citizens choose a document to bring; others add a share code.

  2. 2

    Open Right to Work and press Record check. The method starts from the driver’s own answers.

  3. 3

    Tick what you saw, add the copies or the Home Office profile, then press Pass when nothing is missing.

  4. 4

    Move them to Training, then Promote. Any rota day past a follow-up date is held back, and listed.

Do British drivers need a share code?

No. British and Irish citizens bring an original document instead, usually a passport, and an owner checks it in person. Only other nationalities give a share code. Either way, the check is recorded before Training.

Should we re-check drivers we took on before 1 October?

You don’t have to: they keep working on a Legacy record. You can still record a proper check at any time; for a British passport it’s a one-off. Before setting Block legacy drivers from, read the dry run: it uses each driver’s old recorded expiry, which may be a passport’s own date.
